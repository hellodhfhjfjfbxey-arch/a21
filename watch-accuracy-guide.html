Watch Accuracy

No watch is perfect. Knowing what is normal, and what affects accuracy, helps you keep yours on time.

:12

Seconds per day

Accuracy is measured as the seconds a watch gains or loses in a day. Quartz watches typically drift by around 15 seconds a month. Many mechanical watches run within plus or minus 10 to 20 seconds a day, and the best are within a few seconds.

:24

What is normal

  • Quartz: about half a second a day.
  • Good mechanical: within about 10 seconds a day.
  • Chronometer standard: roughly minus 4 to plus 6 seconds a day for the movement.
:36

What affects a mechanical watch

Position, temperature, magnetism, a low power reserve and shocks all change the rate slightly. A watch left dial-up overnight may run a little differently from one left crown-down. Strong magnets, such as in speakers or bag clasps, can make a watch run very fast.

:48

Simple ways to improve it

Wear it regularly or keep it wound, so the mainspring stays in its most consistent range. Experiment with overnight resting positions to balance gains and losses. Keep it away from magnets, and if it suddenly gains minutes a day, ask a watchmaker to demagnetise it.

:60

When to see a watchmaker

If the rate changes suddenly, the watch stops when worn, or it has not been serviced in many years, a watchmaker can clean, lubricate and regulate it. Regulation alone is a small job; a full service restores the whole movement.
