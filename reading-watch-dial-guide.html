Guide 01

Reading a Watch Dial

Every analogue dial follows the same few rules. Learn them once and any watch, plain or busy, becomes easy to read.

:12

The hour and minute hands

The short hand shows the hour and moves slowly between numerals as the hour passes. The long hand shows minutes and should reach the minute track at the edge of the dial. Halfway between three and four, the hour hand tells you it is about half past three.

:24

The minute track

Most dials have sixty small marks around the edge. Each numeral or index sits on every fifth mark, so the minute hand on the four means twenty minutes past. Count the small marks beyond it for the exact minute.

:36

Indices instead of numbers

  • Batons: simple bars at each hour, often with a double bar at twelve.
  • Roman numerals: traditional; four may appear as IIII.
  • No markers: read by the angle; twelve is always at the top.
:48

Seconds and small dials

A long, thin central hand sweeping fast is the seconds hand. A small sub-dial near six o’clock may show running seconds instead. Other sub-dials might show a date, a second time zone or a chronograph.

:60

Saying the time

Read the hour first, then the minutes: 4:20 is twenty past four; 4:45 is quarter to five. Twenty-four-hour time adds twelve after noon, so twenty past four in the afternoon is 16:20.
